Do architecturally distinct AI and quantitative strategies converge on the same failure mode during stress? This paper examines four architectures -- execution-layer AI (JPMorgan), factor-covariance risk modelling (BlackRock/Aladdin), risk parity (Bridgewater), and multi-factor systematic strategies (Two Sigma) -- during the March 2020 COVID drawdown and August 2024 yen carry unwind. In 2020, pairwise correlations nearly doubled (0.25 to 0.50) and event-window regressions showed significant negative lagged spread coefficients for AGG (p=0.006), HYG (p<0.001), and MTUM (p=0.005). In 2024, correlations rose only 19%, no strategy showed the same signature, and the Bridgewater replicator gained +0.9% versus -15.7% in 2020. The credit-liquidity channel explains correlated failure in 2020 and its absence in 2024. Architectural diversity did not prevent convergence; the common-mode channel operated at the funding-market level, not the model level.
